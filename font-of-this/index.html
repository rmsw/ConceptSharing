Rainmaker Software — Logo Concepts

Four directions for the trademark. All marks are defined with currentColor, so the accent color is inherited from the surrounding text.

Each concept is shown at favicon (16 px), inline-text (1.35 em inside a real "Authored by" line), small (64 px), and marketing (192 px) sizes, on dark and light backgrounds. Source files are the 0N-*.svg files in this folder.

Concept E — Raindrop Eye (A × D — raindrop as pupil)

Concept A's raindrop re-cast as the pupil inside a horizontal ellipse — A's orbit ring (r=13) stretched flat (ry=6.5) into an eyelid. The teardrop's tip is truncated by a short upward-bulging cubic that mirrors the upper lid's curvature, so the drop sits just under the lid without piercing it and still reads as a raindrop.

Concept F — Raindrop Eye, tucked (E, clipped by the lid + gravity-heavy bulb)

Iteration on E. The drop no longer floats inside the lid with a visible gap: it's clipped by the lid ellipse so its pointed tip disappears behind the upper lid — the staircase-as-eye reading. The drop is also reshaped to be gravity-heavy rather than egg-shaped: a tapering tip with a fat round bulb (r=4.25) that kisses the lid's inner lower edge. Concept A's satellite dot on the right is restored as the orbit point.

Concept G — Raindrop Eye, pointy (F, with an exponential 1/x tip)

Iteration on F. The top of the drop is redrawn with a near-vertical, exponentially-tapered profile (cubic control points sit on the y-axis at x≈16.05, so the sides stay pressed against the centreline until very late, then fan out sharply toward the shoulder — a 1/x-like shape). The tip is razor-sharp at y=3; most of it is hidden behind the upper lid, and what peeks out below the lid "opens up" toward the centre of the eye, where the lid arches highest. Visible pupil height grows from ~0 at the outer corner to ~11 svg-units dead-centre, mirroring a real iris-behind-eyelid reading. Same gravity-heavy bulb and satellite dot as F.

Concept H — Raindrop Eye, squished (G, flattened lid + narrower, pointier, more-hidden drop)

Iteration on G. The lid is squished flatter (ry 6.5 → 4.5, ~31% thinner), the drop is reshaped to be narrower and taller with an even sharper tip (shoulder width halved from 4.25 to 2.75; tip raised from y=3 to y=1, so ~10 svg-units of tip hide behind the lid), and the bulb is correspondingly smaller (r=2.75) so it still kisses the lid's inner lower edge. Same exponential 1/x tip philosophy as G — first cubic control point hugs the y-axis at x≈16.05. Visible pupil height at the centre is now only ~7.5 (was ~11 in G) and falls to zero by x=18.75; more of the drop is hidden, less peeks through, and the reveal still opens up toward the middle of the eye.

Concept I — Ellipse Eye (G's lid, pupil is a horizontal ellipse instead of a raindrop)

Starting fresh from Concept G's lid. The raindrop pupil is replaced with another horizontal ellipse — concentric with the lid, slightly narrower (rx 7.5 vs lid inner 12.25) and slightly taller (ry 7 vs lid inner 5.75). The lid's upper and lower arcs clip the pupil's top and bottom symmetrically, so the iris naturally "tucks under" the lid the way an eyelid frames a real iris — no pointy seams, no awkward clip geometry. At the centre column the pupil fills the lid's full interior (11.5 svg-units tall); the reveal tapers smoothly to zero at x=8.5 and x=23.5, leaving clean sclera at the corners for the satellite dot to sit in. Lid stroke and satellite dot are identical to G, so F/G/H/I share the same outer silhouette.

Concept J — Ellipse Eye, elongated (I with more-elliptic pupil, shifted up so the top lid hides more)

Iteration on I. The pupil is made more elliptic — rx 7.5 → 9 while ry stays at 7, aspect ratio 1.07 → 1.29 — and shifted up one unit (cy 16 → 15). The upper lid now does most of the hiding: ~25.7 svg² of pupil area is clipped by the upper lid vs only ~1.1 svg² by the lower lid (≈24× asymmetry), matching how a real upper eyelid sits heavier on an iris than the lower lid does. The centre column still reveals the full 11.5 svg-units of lid interior, and the pupil still kisses the bottom lid at y=21.75, so the iris keeps its grounded "resting on the lower lid" weight. Lid stroke and satellite dot are unchanged, so F/G/H/I/J share the same outer silhouette.

Concept L — Asymmetric Droplet Eye (pupil like the Rainmaker profile picture — flat on top, round on bottom)

A different take on the asymmetry: rather than imposing it by clipping a symmetric ellipse with the lid (as in J/K), the pupil itself is an asymmetric droplet — a path made of two half-ellipses joined along the horizontal diameter at y=16.25. The top half has ry=3.5 (flat crown, as if compressed by the upper eyelid); the bottom half has ry=5.5 (round belly, weight resting on the lower lid). rx=7 on both halves. Result: a small pupil (≈50% of J's area, aspect 1.56) with 2.5 svg-units of sclera above — the same big dark crescent you see under the heavy upper lid in the Rainmaker staircase-eye profile picture — and a clean kiss on the lower lid at y=21.75. Lid and satellite dot unchanged, so F/G/H/I/J/K/L all share the same outer silhouette.

Concept M — Ellipse Eye, small & flatter (K flattened; the new empty space is applied below so the top lid still covers the same; satellite upgraded to a sharp raindrop breaking the lid line)

Evolution of K. The pupil is flattened by 1 svg-unit (ry 5.5 → 4.5) and all of the resulting 2 svg-units of new empty space are applied below the pupil, by shifting cy up by exactly the same amount (15 → 14). The pupil's top edge therefore stays pinned at y=9.5, so the upper lid still clips exactly 0.75 svg-units off the crown — identical top-lid coverage to K. The bottom rises from y=20.5 to y=18.5, leaving 3.25 svg-units of sclera above the lower lid (K had 1.25). Aspect widens from 1.45 to 1.78; area is ≈82% of K and ≈57% of J. Reads as an even calmer, more recessed iris with a thicker "sill" of bare lid below. The right-hand satellite is now a very sharp raindrop: a semicircular bulb of radius 1.3 centred at (28, 16) puts all the visual weight INSIDE the lid (pointing inward toward the pupil), while the tip at (31, 16) — ≈53° — pokes OUTSIDE the lid, 2 svg-units past its right edge at x=29, literally breaking the lid line outward. Lid unchanged (same outer ellipse as F/G/H/I/J/K/L/N).

Concept N — Asymmetric Droplet Eye, high & left (L shifted up and slightly left so the top lid covers some of the crown)

Evolution of L. The droplet pupil keeps its asymmetric two-half-ellipse shape — flat top (ry=3.5), round bottom (ry=5.5), rx=7 on both halves — but is shifted by (dx=−1, dy=−3.25): new centre cx=15 with the joint at y=13 (L was 16 / 16.25). Concept O — Lazy Eye, Open Orbit, Razor Raindrop (mild: three-axis push past M — flatter pupil half-tucked into the lid, lid opened ±13° at the right, rounded dot replaced by a cusp-tipped needle)

First step of a three-tier escalation past M along three simultaneous axes. Lazy eye: pupil flattened ry 4.5 → 4 and shifted up cy 14 → 13.5 so its top edge stays pinned at y=9.5 but its bottom rises 18.5 → 17.5 and a bit more of the crown hides behind the upper-lid stroke; aspect widens 1.78 → 2.00. Open orbit: the lid is no longer a closed ellipse — it is drawn as an arc with a ±13° angular gap at its rightmost point, endpoints on the math ellipse at (28.67, 14.54) and (28.67, 17.46), so the ring literally opens where the drop exits. Razor raindrop: same bulb as M (cx=28, r=1.3) but the tip has been redrawn as a mathematical cusp. The needle is two cubic Béziers from each shoulder to the tip at (31.2, 16); for each cubic the control point adjacent to the tip is placed exactly on the y=16 axis at (28, 16), so both halves arrive at the tip with horizontal tangents pointing in +x — interior angle 0°. Half-width at Δx=0.5 svg from the tip ≈ 0.04 svg: asymptotically sharp versus M's shallow quadratic point.

Concept R — Lazy Eye, Open Orbit, Smooth-Neck Raindrop (O pupil + P gap + P raindrop, with the bulb-to-needle seam fixed by matching curvature at the shoulder)

The user-preferred combination after the O/P/Q round, with P's "seam" diagnosed and fixed. Pupil from O: rx=8, ry=4, cy=13.5 — top edge pinned at y=9.5, bottom at 17.5, aspect 2.00; upper lid clips ≈0.75 svg off the crown (same lazy read as O). Orbit gap from P: ±18° at the right, endpoints on the math ellipse at (28.37, 13.99) and (28.37, 18.01). Raindrop — seam diagnosed as a curvature (C²) discontinuity, not a tangent one. At the shoulder (27.5, 17.3) the circle's tangent is already horizontal (perpendicular to the vertical radius there) and P's cubic left horizontally too — so the tangents match. What jumped was the curvature: circle κ = 1/1.3 ≈ 0.77; P's cubic started at κ ≈ 3.47 (P1 only 0.5 svg east of the shoulder → small initial velocity → big initial bending), so the silhouette pinched ~4.5× sharper than the arc just past the shoulder — that's the read as a kink. The fix is a single +0.5 svg shift: move each cubic's first control point from (28, 17.3) to (28.5, 17.3) (and mirror for the upper side). That doubles the initial velocity, which quarters the initial curvature to ≈ 0.87 — within 13% of the circle's 0.77, visually seamless. Cusp at the tip preserved (control point adjacent to the tip stays on the y=16 axis at (27.5, 16)), so the needle stays exactly as sharp as P's.
